Every day along India’s coastline, fishermen head out to sea relying on guesswork—burning up to 60% of their trip cost on fuel while searching for fish, or unknowingly entering rough waters. At the same time, raw satellite ocean data is too complex for a fisherman or coastal operator to read. ORCA—Oceanic Research and Cognitive Assistant—bridges that gap by turning multi-satellite ocean physics and machine learning into a real-time, multilingual 3D navigation and fishing intelligence system.

Under the hood, ORCA’s model works in three stages. First is Multi-Source Ocean Ingestion. Across 568 active grid nodes in the Indian Exclusive Economic Zone, ORCA ingests physical ocean telemetry from Copernicus Marine and Sentinel-3 satellite arrays, combined with live coastal harbour weather from Open-Meteo. Instead of looking at just one parameter, the model tracks five core physical drivers: Sea Surface Temperature and thermal anomalies, Chlorophyll-a plankton density which is the primary food source for fish, Sea Level Anomaly and surface swell, Salinity, and Dissolved Oxygen.

Second is Anomaly Detection and Multi-Horizon Forecasting. Our backend runs an Isolation Forest anomaly detector alongside gradient-boosted forecasting models trained on multi-year Arabian Sea datasets from 2022 to 2025. The model detects thermal upwelling fronts—where cool, nutrient-rich deep water rises to the surface and triggers plankton blooms that attract fish schools—and computes a 0-to-100 Marine Health and Fishing Suitability Score with a 7-day trend forecast.

Third is 3-Tier Potential Fishing Zone Classification and the 12 Nautical Mile Regulatory Filter. Every ocean node is classified into Prime cyan beacons, Moderate amber beacons, or Low Activity slate nodes. Crucially, ORCA enforces a strict 12-Nautical-Mile, or 22.2-kilometer, regulatory boundary: coastal waters inside the cyan dashed line are reserved exclusively for traditional small artisanal boats, so ORCA only recommends commercial fishing grounds safely beyond 12 nautical miles.

Finally, to make this science usable by anyone at sea, ORCA features a 12-language interface and Cognitive Voice Agent supporting English, Hindi, Hinglish, Marathi, Gujarati, Malayalam, Tamil, Kannada, and more. When a fisherman clicks the microphone and asks in their own language where the best fishing spot is from their harbour, our live voice dock transcribes their speech simultaneously right below the bar. The AI queries the live ocean model with zero hallucination and returns ranked, 12-NM-compliant fishing zones. With one click on "Take me there," ORCA flies the 3D globe or 2D chart directly to the target zone and plots a safe seaward boat route with exact compass heading, distance, and estimated travel time—turning satellite physics and machine learning into actionable guidance in the fisherman's own voice and language.

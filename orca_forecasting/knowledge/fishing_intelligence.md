# Potential Fishing Zones (PFZ) & Fisheries Oceanography

## How ORCA Calculates Fishing Suitability
ORCA does **not** claim to spot individual fish from space. Instead, it models **oceanographic habitat suitability and forage aggregation fronts** based on proven fisheries oceanography:
1. **Thermal Sweet Spot (25 pts)**: Surface temperature within the optimal physiological envelope of target commercial species (typically 26.5°C to 29.2°C in the tropical Arabian Sea).
2. **Thermal Fronts & Upwelling (25 pts)**: Negative temperature anomalies (SSTA between -0.2°C and -1.2°C) indicating ascending cold nutrient-rich waters.
3. **Phytoplankton Forage Bloom (25 pts)**: Chlorophyll-a concentrations between 0.5 and 3.5 mg/m³, representing grazing grounds for zooplankton, which attract schooling baitfish.
4. **Current Shear & Cyclonic Divergence (15 pts)**: Current velocity between 0.15 and 0.60 m/s and negative sea level anomalies (cyclonic eddies) that physically concentrate prey.
5. **Ecosystem Health (10 pts)**: Marine Health Index >= 55/100, ensuring waters are oxygenated and not experiencing lethal thermal stress.

## Target Pelagic Species in the Arabian Sea
- **Indian Mackerel (*Rastrelliger kanagurta*)**: Thrives along coastal thermal fronts and moderate chlorophyll zones (0.8 to 2.5 mg/m³). Highly abundant along the Konkan and Malabar coasts.
- **Oil Sardines (*Sardinella longiceps*)**: Highly dependent on coastal upwelling fronts and rich diatom blooms. Feeds directly on phytoplankton and zooplankton.
- **Yellowfin Tuna (*Thunnus albacares*)**: Pelagic predator found along sharp thermal boundaries and open-water drop-offs where baitfish concentrate.
- **Squid (*Uroteuthis duvaucelii*)**: Found around shelf breaks with strong current convergence and nocturnal forage movement.

## Why is Upwelling Critical for Fishing?
- In tropical oceans, sunlight warms the surface, creating a warm, light upper layer that prevents deeper, heavy, nutrient-rich water from mixing upward (stratification).
- Wind-driven **upwelling** (Ekman transport) pushes the surface water offshore, drawing cold water rich in nitrates, phosphates, and silicates up from depths of 100-200 meters into the sunlit euphotic zone.
- Within 24-72 hours, this influx of nutrients triggers explosive diatom growth (chlorophyll surge), attracting zooplankton, schooling small pelagics, and apex pelagic predators. Upwelling zones are nature's ocean cafeterias.

## Fish Species Policy
ORCA calculates habitat suitability rather than detecting specific fish species via satellite. When pelagic species are mentioned, they reflect historical biological associations with those specific temperature and chlorophyll forage regimes.
